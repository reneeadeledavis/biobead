Overview

A 98-pot greenhouse study testing 14 treatments over 16 weeks on wheat with root exclusion zones, isotope dosing, GHG & nutrient leachate measurements, and microbial community analysis.

Treatments

  1. BioBead – bacteria (blue)

  2. BioBead – AMF (pink)

  3. BioBead - bacteria, AMF (purple)

  4. SL-primed BioBead - bacteria, AMF (white)

  5. SL-primed BioBead - bacteria (orange)

  6. SL-primed BioBead - AMF (green)

  7. BioBead w/ reapplication at week 8 (blue, underdosed)

  8. ½ fertilizer ½ BioBead (pink)

  9. Liquid consortia (purpe)

  10. Commercial biofertilizer (Hardygro inoculum, white)

  11. Empty bead (yellow)

  12. SL-primed empty bead (green)

  13. Synthetic fertilizer (positive control – 10% dose, 0.6 g/L, 10mL/pot)

  14. Untreated (neg control, yellow)

Design: 7 reps per treatment (98 pots).

Wheat variety: Red Fife (Territorial).

Formulation details

Equal mix of G. mossea, R. irregularis, R. intraradices spores.

Bacterial consortia:

  • 6 uL P. graminis

  • 6 uL P. rhizosphereae

  • 6 uL Azospirillum brasiliense

  • 6 uL Azotobacter vinelandii

  • 6 uL Rhanella spp.

  • 6 uL Phylobacter spp.

5 nM GR24 for SL treatments.

Measurements & protocols

In-experiment

  • PRS probes (Western Ag, triplicate per tx) o 64 probe pairs (cation/anion) o Using half of the treatments: we have 24 pots to be treated…want 1 analysis per pot o 2 cation, 2 anion probes per pot o 24 complete analyses

  • 2% 15N isotope dosing during stem elongation (2 rounds/timepoints, triplicate per tx). Designate 3 pots/reps per treatment. Leave 3 additional reps as untreated NA controls o Stem elongation/week 4. Follow up treatment week 5.

  • Leachate: NO₃⁻, NH₄⁺, PO₄³⁻ (days 14, 28, 42, 70, 98/harvest) Water each pot heavily (1000 mL). Collect 50 mL leachate from the collection tray from all 7 replicates. Analyze on the Gallery for NO₃⁻, NH₄⁺, PO₄³⁻ (include water blank) (Note: freezing impacts P, so do not freeze samples.)

  • GHG emissions: CO₂, N₂O (days 14, 28, 42). Rake a gas sample, inject it in a sealed serum bottle and then inject when time allows.

  • Moisture readings (every 2 weeks, starting week 2).

End of experiment

  • 15N uptake: root, above ground biomass, stem, grain, soil, root exclusion soil

  • Wheat grain yield

  • Root staining

  • Soil metagenomes

  • GeoChip analysis

Root exclusion pilot

Goal: Test design + durability of 3 root exclusion prototypes & 2 mesh sizes in soil-rich conditions for 4 weeks. Designs: 1. Tie bag 2. Tie bag w/ PVC elbow 3. Pot sectioned with sheet Mesh: 25 μm and 37 μm Setup:

  • 6 x 1-gallon pots: one of each combo

  • Materials: mesh, tie bags, PVC, BioBeads, AMF inoculum, 10 gallons soil, 36 wheat seedlings. Assessment: 3rd week of July (after 4 weeks). Check: root penetration, mesh stability, fungal colonization.

Results: A single replicate of the mesh prototypes was examined for root interactions. All seams were intact. Roots were observed climbing up through the bag ties. Generally, evidence of root adherence to the outside of the bags was observed. Wheat roots were not found inside the root exclusion zones. Under a dissecting microscope, root hairs were found on the inside of the 37-micron mesh material. Adherence of roots to 25-micron mesh was noted, but root material was not observed on the inside. While the seams were intact over a 4-week period, it is possible that roots could make their way through over a longer burial duration. This could be a failure point that is not captured in a short term pilot study. Hence, 2 replicates of the mesh prototypes were left in pots to grow longer while the study is being prepared. Based on these results and ease of use, it is recommended that a single piece of 37 mesh should be used in the larger study to section off the root exclusion zone. This is the safest route. If having root hairs inside the mesh is a blocking concern, then the 25-micron tie bags can be used instead. This study used 25-micron mesh tied up in a ball and sealed with twine. 1 was applied in the center of each pot.

Strigolactone priming

Strigolactones (SLs) are plant-produced hormones that regulate root branching and shoot development in plants. SLs also initiate the mycorrhizal symbiosis process by promoting AMF spore germination and hyphal branching through mitochondrial stimulation (Besserer et al, 2008). SLs can be applied as foliar sprays or root drenches. The synthetic SL analog GR24 (298.29 g/mol) has been used in agricultural studies, including wheat plants. SLs can have varying stereochemistry. The natural, plant-produce SLs are (+) enantiomers, while the synthetically produced GR24 is a racemic mixture of (+) and (-) enantiomers. Therefore, the best synthetic analog to purchase is (+)-GR24, but the racemic mixture (+/-) is accessible if the former is cost-prohibitive.
SLs are active at low concentrations, from the micro to pico molar range. 5 and 10 µM are concentrations used in studies on foliar sprays for wheat plants, particularly in the context of drought and salinity stress (Sedaghat et al, 2017, Sedaghat et al, 2021, Jariana et al, 2025). However, in vitro experiments with GR24 and AMF spore responses use lower concentrations (10 nM, Besserer et al, 2008). GR24 is soluble in several organic solvents including ethanol, methanol, DMSO, and DMF. The Sedaghat group dissolved GR24 in acetone, then diluted with distilled water to get their desired concentration and then applied it as a foliar spray. The Besserer group had the same approach: initial dissolve in acetone, then dilution with water. This yields a 0.0001%, acetone solution, which should not be harmful to bacteria. GR24 is temperature- sensitive and should be stored at 4C (or -20C). The following methods are suggested for this study:

  • 10 nM concentration target (becomes 5 nM after addition of 4% Na-alg)

  • Dissolution in ethanol to 10-2 M, then dilute in sterile MilliQ to reach 10-8 /10E-9 M (10 nM)

  • Keep under refrigeration

  1. Making a 10⁻² M (10 mM) stock solution of GR24 in ethanol:

Mass of GR24 = 1 mg; molecular weight (MW) = 298.29 g/mol

  • Target concentration =10⁻² M = 10 mM

  • moles = 1 mg/298.29 mg/mmol = 0.00335mmol

Vol = moles/molarity = 0.00335mmol/10mmol/L = 0.000335L = 335 μL

Dissolve 2 mg GR24 in 670 µL ethanol to make a 10 mM (10⁻² M) solution.

2. Making a 10⁻⁸ M (10 nM) working solution in water:

  • Going from 10⁻² M to 10⁻⁸ M is a 1,000,000-fold dilution:

10⁻² M → 10⁻⁴ M (1:100 dilution; 1 µL stock + 99 µL water)

10⁻⁴ M → 10⁻⁶ M (1:100 dilution)

10⁻⁶ M → 10⁻⁸ M (1:100 dilution)
